# B2 | Water and food in the field | BRAVO · based on ATP 3-50.21 and AFH 10-644

## B2.1 | Advanced water procurement | web
- **Transpiration bag:** a clear bag tied over a living, sunny, leafy branch; the plant can give one or two cups of water a day. Never on a poisonous plant.
- **Solar still:** a hole about 1 m wide, a container in the middle, plastic sheet over it with a small stone in the centre. Little water for a lot of work – best for purifying salty or dirty water.
- **Dew:** wipe the grass with a cloth at dawn, then wring it out.
- **Plants:** birch and maple sap in spring; a cut wild grapevine drips water.
- **Snow and ice:** melt it, don't eat it (it chills and dehydrates you). Put a little water in the bottom of the pot so it doesn't scorch.
! Purify all natural water: boiling, tablets, or filter + disinfection. Filtering alone does not kill pathogens.

## B2.2 | Edible and poisonous plants | web
Only eat what you **recognise for certain**. Plant food gives little energy, but provides vitamins and fibre.
- **Safe and easy to recognise (Central Europe):** nettle (cooked), dandelion, plantain, sorrel, wild raspberry, blackberry, rose hips, hazelnuts, acorns (leached in several changes of water), inner bark of pine, pine-needle tea.
- **Avoid:** plants with milky sap, a bitter-almond smell, umbrella-shaped flower heads (hemlock!), leaves in threes, and white or yellow berries.
- **Never eat mushrooms** in the field – the deadly species can be mistaken for edible ones.
- **Yew:** every part is deadly poisonous (including the seed of the red berry) – never make tea from it.
**Edibility test (only in an emergency, if there's nothing else for days):** one plant part, first on the skin, then the lip, the tongue, then a small bite, and wait 8 hours. Slow, and it doesn't protect against every poison.

## B2.3 | Animal food | web
- **Insects:** the easiest protein to get (grubs, grasshoppers, ant pupae). Always boil or roast them. Avoid hairy, brightly coloured, stinging and bad-smelling ones.
- **Fish:** improvised hook (needle, thorn), line, bait; a fish trap in shallow water; always cook fish thoroughly.
- **Small game with snares:** on game trails, at narrow points, in front of burrows. Many snares, checked at least once a day.
! In peacetime, hunting, trapping and fishing require a licence. These methods are for genuine survival situations only.
+ The body can last weeks without food, but only days without water. Getting food must not cost more energy than it gives.

## B2.4 | Preparing and storing food | web
- Clean animals away from the water source and your camp; bury the remains (smell, predators, tracks).
- Cook everything thoroughly: the best protection against parasites and bacteria.
- **Preserving:** cut into thin strips and smoke them, or dry them in the sun and wind.
- Boiling keeps most nutrients – drink the broth too.
- In hostile territory the smell and smoke of cooking give you away: eat cold food there if you can.
