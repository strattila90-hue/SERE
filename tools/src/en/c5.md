# 5 | Evasion | Part II, Lesson 1 — based on the course outline, extended from public sources

## 5.1 | Purpose of evasion | web
Evasion is the set of procedures by which the isolated person avoids detection and contact by hostile or potentially hostile forces and gets back to friendly forces or is extracted.
+ Captivity is worse than evasion! Every decision aims to stay unseen and return.
- First step after isolation: leave the landing/accident site immediately – that's where they look first.
- Take only the necessary gear; hide or destroy the rest.
- Move to a hide, then S-T-O-P: stop, think, observe, plan.

## 5.2 | Hiding and alternative destinations | web
- Choose a hide by the **“LOW” principle**: low silhouette, odd (irregular) shape, wild/blends into the terrain – not the obvious bush or building.
- Primary destination: designated extraction site / friendly forces. Alternatives: secondary extraction point, neutral territory, border, allied forces.
- Always have a plan B: escape route, rally point.

## 5.3 | Camouflage
Camouflage aims to make the isolated person, their gear and their shelter unrecognisable to the enemy's senses and sensors. Camouflage is an active task – it must be maintained continuously.

## 5.4 | Types of observation | web
**The five senses:** the enemy sees, hears, smells (tobacco, soap, cooking, fire), feels (tracks) and tastes – guard against all of them.
**Direct observation:**
- Physical signs: footprints, broken branches, trampled grass, discarded litter, traces of fire.
- Locals: dogs, shepherds, hunters, farmers – they are the best “sensors”.
**Indirect observation:** aerial and drone reconnaissance (“the view from above” – regular shapes, shadows and paths stand out), thermal and night-vision devices, radio direction finding.

## 5.5 | Recognition factors | web
What the human eye spots immediately – eliminate these:
1. **Movement** – the biggest giveaway; move slowly and deliberately, freeze when observed.
2. **Position** – not on the skyline, not on prominent points, not in the obvious spot.
3. **Shape** – break up the outline of the human body, helmet and weapon.
4. **Shadow** – your own shadow gives you away; use nature's deep shadows.
5. **Texture** – smooth surfaces look lighter and shine; rough surfaces look darker.
6. **Colour** – mottled colouring that matches the surroundings.
7. **Tone** – differences in brightness; the glowing patch of face and hands.
8. **Shine** – watch, glasses, metal, optics, sweaty skin – cover them, use matt surfaces.
+ Memory aid: **movement – shape – shadow – shine – silhouette – spacing**.

## 5.6 | Principles of camouflage | web
- **Site:** fits the surroundings, inconspicuous, several exits.
- **Camouflage discipline:** leave no tracks or litter; avoid paths.
- **Light discipline:** no light at all at night (torch, match, screen, cigarette) – visible from far away.
- **Against night vision and thermal:** hide body heat behind soil, rocks, dense vegetation or an emergency blanket (used correctly); not in open ground.
- **Noise discipline:** secure your gear, whisper, hand signals, don't snap branches.
- **Concealed shelter:** irregular shape, local material, hidden from aerial view too.

## 5.7 | Personal camouflage | web
- **Shape:** break up your outline with local vegetation (on clothing, helmet, pack); change it as the surroundings change.
- **Background:** always match the background – dark clothing stands out against a light background.
- **Techniques:** face paint: raised areas (forehead, nose, cheekbones, chin) darker, hollows lighter; hands and neck too.
- **Blending:** before moving, check how you fit the surroundings; move slowly and irregularly.

## 5.8 | Concealment in temperate zones | web
- On clothing: fresh, local vegetation – not all one kind, not too much.
- Natural materials: mud, soil, charcoal for the face; leaves, moss.
- For shelters: irregular shape, local material, no straight edges; covered from above as well.
- **Fire while hiding:** only if essential; small smokeless fire (dry hardwood), Dakota fire hole, under the canopy; by day smoke and by night light give you away.

## 5.9 | Getting food during evasion | web
- Concealment comes first – foraging is a risk.
- Animals: traps and snares (quiet, passive); hunting or firing a weapon makes noise – last resort only.
- Plants: only species you know are edible; if in doubt, don't eat.
- In inhabited areas: avoid taking anything that will be missed; abandoned gardens and orchards – carefully.
- Preparation: clean and cook raw animal food and water.

## 5.10 | Security and movement | web
**Precautions:** stop, look, listen and smell regularly (STOP–LOOK–LISTEN–SMELL). Before moving, plan the route and the next hide.
**Observing terrain:** even without binoculars: sector by sector, near to far; watch the skyline and the shadows.
**Main reasons evaders get caught:** risk-taking from impatience, fatigue, hunger or thirst; using roads, bridges and villages; tracks; asking unreliable people for help.
**Movement:**
- Use poor light (dawn, dusk, night).
- Route: avoid roads, paths, ridgelines and open ground; move below the crest, through dips, along the edge of vegetation.
- Hide tracks: hard ground, stones, water's edge; step in already trampled spots.
- Reduce the chance of discovery: short bounds, frequent stops, changes of direction.
**Ways of moving:** stalking walk, running (only if detected), crawling (low, high, side).

## 5.11 | Rally points | web
A pre-designated, easily recognisable terrain feature where the group regroups after being scattered.
- Recognisable but not conspicuous; with a set waiting time and an onward plan.
- At the rally point: security, head count, treat the wounded, assess, plan.
- Obstacles: rivers, roads, fences, towns, minefields – cross them only when justified and after reconnaissance.

## 5.12 | Evasion aids | web
- **Personal survival kit (PSK):** fire starter, water purification, signalling devices, compass, knife, cord, first aid, food, map – always on your body.
- **Maps and charts:** evasion chart (with blood chit), code tables.
- **Bilingual phrase card (“Pointee-Talkee”):** picture-and-text sheet in the local language for communicating by pointing.
- **Assisted evasion:** an organised, vetted network of helpers.
- **Unexpected help:** a local helper – high risk, only when justified.
- **Contact with locals:** only as a last resort, with someone alone, after observing; don't reveal more than necessary.
