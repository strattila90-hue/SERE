# B3 | Advanced navigation | BRAVO · based on ATP 3-50.21 and FM 3-25.26

## B3.1 | Navigating by the stars | web
- **North Star (northern hemisphere):** find the Big Dipper and measure five times the distance between the two pointer stars at the end of the “pan” – there is the North Star, and north is on the horizon below it.
- **Cassiopeia:** the “W”-shaped constellation opposite the Big Dipper, on the other side of the North Star – useful when the Dipper is hidden.
- **Orion's Belt:** rises in the east, sets in the west; the line of three stars runs roughly east–west.
**Star-movement method:** line up two sticks on a star and check after 15–20 minutes which way it moved. Up → you're facing east, down → west, right → south, left → north.

## B3.2 | Sun and watch | web
- **Shadow stick:** push a stick into the ground, mark the tip of the shadow; mark it again after 15 minutes. The first mark is west, the second east; the line between them runs east–west.
- **Analogue watch (northern hemisphere):** point the hour hand at the sun; halfway between the hour hand and 12 is south. During summer time use 1 instead of 12.
- **At local noon** the sun is due south, and the shadow is shortest and points north.
! Moss, tree rings and anthills are not reliable direction indicators – don't rely on them.

## B3.3 | Dead reckoning and distance | web
**Dead reckoning:** from a known point you travel a measured distance on a known bearing (azimuth), constantly working out where you are.
- **Pace count:** measure your own value over 100 m beforehand (flat, slope, dense vegetation separately). Mark every 100 m (a knot in a cord, a pebble in your pocket).
- **Bypassing an obstacle:** turn out at a right angle counting paces, continue on the original bearing, then come back to the line with the same number of paces.
- **Aiming off:** if you're looking for a point on a linear feature (road, stream), deliberately aim to one side of it – then you'll know which way to turn when you reach it.
- At night and in fog, distances seem longer and your pace is shorter – allow for it.

## B3.4 | Reading the terrain | web
- **Handrail:** a linear feature (stream, ridge, forest edge, power line) you can follow with certainty, without constantly checking the compass.
- **Catching feature:** a landmark beyond your objective; if you reach it, you know you've gone too far.
- **Ridge, saddle, valley, spur:** the shapes of contour lines – practise matching the map to the real terrain.
- If you're unsure: stop (S-T-O-P), go back to the last certain point, don't guess.
+ In the field, water running downhill (stream → river) usually leads to settlements and roads – but when evading, those are exactly the dangerous places.
