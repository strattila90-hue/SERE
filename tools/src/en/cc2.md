# C2 | Rights and conduct of a prisoner of war | CHARLIE · based on the Third Geneva Convention and AFH 10-644

## C2.1 | Protection of the Third Geneva Convention | web
A soldier captured in an international armed conflict is a **prisoner of war**, protected by the Third Geneva Convention (1949).
- Entitled to humane treatment; torture, intimidation and public humiliation are prohibited.
- Entitled to food, water, clothing, accommodation and medical care.
- May correspond with family and be visited by the International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC).
- **Under questioning, only four items must be given:** surname and first names, rank, date of birth, service number. No other information may be forced out of a prisoner (Article 17).
! Not every adversary respects the Convention. Knowing your rights still gives you strength, and it is the basis for holding them accountable later.

## C2.2 | You remain a soldier in captivity | web
- Captivity does not end military discipline: the senior prisoner leads, the others support.
- Help your comrades; don't accept favours that harm them.
- Don't make statements or sign anything that could be used against your country, the allies or your comrades.
- Keep your human dignity: washing, order, bearing – these are part of survival.

## C2.3 | Interrogation – general principles | web
The aim of interrogation is to gain information and often propaganda.
- Stick to the four items and deflect everything else politely and calmly.
- Methods vary: kindness, promises, fatigue, isolation, misleading “information” about your comrades. Expect them and don't be surprised.
- Don't believe that “the others have already told us everything”.
- If you slip, don't give up: go back to your starting position at the next question.
+ Specific resistance techniques are practised with level C course instructors under controlled conditions.

## C2.4 | Peacetime detention and hostage situations | web
During peace operations, missions or travel abroad, a detained person is **not a prisoner of war**, so the situation is different.
- The first minutes are the most dangerous: don't resist armed people, follow instructions.
- Stay human in your captors' eyes: name, family, calm voice – don't be submissive, but don't provoke either.
- Eat, drink and sleep when you can; keep your routine.
- During a rescue: **lie down**, hands visible, don't run, don't grab anything, follow the rescuers' instructions – at first they treat everyone as a suspect.
