# 7 | Extraction – link-up procedures | Part III, Lesson 1 — based on the course outline, extended from public sources

## 7.1 | Why JPR matters | web
Personnel Recovery (PR) is the sum of military, diplomatic and civil efforts to recover and reintegrate isolated personnel. Successful recovery boosts morale, prevents the enemy from using a captured person for propaganda or intelligence, and preserves trained personnel.

## 7.2 | Basic terms | web
- **ISOP (IP):** isolated personnel.
- **JPR:** Joint Personnel Recovery.
- **CSAR:** combat search and rescue – one method of the “Recover” task, not a synonym for PR.
- **JPRC:** Joint Personnel Recovery Centre; **PRCC:** Personnel Recovery Coordination Cell.
- **ISOPREP:** pre-filled identification data sheet (question–answer, photo, fingerprints) for authentication.
- **EPA:** Evasion Plan of Action – the planned route and intentions.

## 7.3 | European and national aspects | web
- **EDA (European Defence Agency):** from 2013 launched personnel recovery planner and controller courses (PRCPC, later JPRSC) to improve interoperability between member states.
- **EPRC (European Personnel Recovery Centre):** established on 8 July 2015, based at Poggio Renatico air base in Italy. An intergovernmental military organisation harmonising PR rules and training.
+ Hungary is active: the Air Command and Control Centre in Veszprém regularly hosts EDA JPR staff courses, with Hungarian instructors.

## 7.4 | Operational elements: JPRC, PRCC | web
- **JPRC:** directs and coordinates all PR activity in the theatre at operational command level.
- **PRCC:** coordinates recovery at component/national level and liaises with the JPRC.
- **Links:** in NATO, EU and UN operations national PR elements plug into the multinational structure; the aim is common procedures and fast help.

## 7.5 | Phases of the JPR system | web
1. **Preparation:** training (SERE), equipment, ISOPREP, planning.
2. **Planning:** PR plans, EPA, communication procedures.
3. **Execution:** the five execution tasks.
4. **Assessment:** processing lessons learned.

## 7.6 | Education and training | web
Personnel recovery rests on three pillars: commanders and staffs, recovery forces, and the **isolated person** themselves. The isolated person's preparedness (SERE, ISOPREP, EPA, equipment) decisively affects the success of recovery.

## 7.7 | The five PR execution tasks | web
1. **Report:** begins with recognising the isolating event; must be fast and accurate.
2. **Locate:** finding and authenticating the person; recovery forces only launch with an accurate position and positive ID.
3. **Support:** supporting the person and their family – two-way communication, resupply drops, suppressing enemy forces.
4. **Recover:** coordinated action of commanders, recovery forces and the isolated person.
5. **Reintegrate:** medical check, debriefing, return to duty and family – physical and mental health come first.
+ The isolated person takes part too: making radio contact helps the Report and Locate tasks.

## 7.8 | International commitments | web
Soldiers serving in NATO and EU operations (e.g. KFOR) work in environments where a PR system is available – SERE preparedness fits into it.

## 7.9 | Purpose of link-up | web
Link-up aims for the isolated person and the recovery/friendly force to meet **safely and with mutual authentication** – without fratricide or falling into an enemy trap.

## 7.10 | Steps before physical contact | web
1. Reach the designated extraction/rally site and observe the surroundings.
2. Check in according to the given procedure (radio, signal) – only on instruction.
3. Prepare signalling devices (mirror, flare, strobe, IR signal, panel).
4. Equipment: weapon on safe, nothing suspicious in your hands.
5. Follow the recovery force's instructions exactly.
! To a recovery force an unknown armed person is a threat – keep your hands visible.

## 7.11 | Authentication | web
- **ISOPREP questions:** pre-set personal questions only the isolated person can answer.
- **Number, letter, colour codes:** according to the daily/operational codes.
- **Sound, hand and light signals:** in a pre-agreed sequence.
- **Checks:** the recovery force may put the isolated person on the ground and search them – this is a security procedure, not hostility.

## 7.12 | Real example – Libya 2011 | web
On 21 March 2011, during Operation Odyssey Dawn, a US F-15E Strike Eagle crashed near Benghazi due to a technical failure. The two crew members ejected but landed far apart.
- The pilot was picked up by a Marine **TRAP** package launched from USS Kearsarge (2 MV-22 Ospreys, 2 CH-53Es, 2 AV-8B Harriers) within about 90 minutes of the report; time on the ground was under 90 seconds.
- The weapon systems officer was found by local rebels and later handed over to US forces.
+ Lesson: fast reporting, accurate positioning and authentication, a recovery force on standby – and a prepared isolated person.
