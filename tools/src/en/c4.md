# 4 | Finding and purifying water | Part I, Lesson 4

## 4.1 | Introduction
Water is one of the most important things for sustaining human life. About **70%** of the body is water; it is essential for circulation, kidney function, breathing, digestion and temperature regulation.
> You can survive weeks without food, but on average only **3 days** without water.
Clean drinking water is not guaranteed in a survival situation – every soldier must know how to obtain, purify and safely store water. Lack of water causes dehydration, confusion, muscle weakness and ultimately death.

## 4.2 | Dehydration
Loss of fluids, which also involves loss of electrolytes. Three main types:
1. **Isotonic:** water and electrolytes are lost in the same proportion. Causes: diarrhoea, vomiting, burns, sweating. Signs: fatigue, flushed skin, headache, dark urine, cramps. Treatment: isotonic fluid replacement.
2. **Hypotonic:** electrolyte deficiency from replacing water without minerals. Sodium deficiency → disturbed nerve conduction, muscle cramps. Signs: weakness, nausea, oedema. Treatment: replace salts and electrolytes, restrict water.
3. **Hypertonic:** lack of fluid – the plasma thickens and draws water from the cells. Signs: strong thirst, dry mucous membranes, confusion. Treatment: gradual, slow rehydration.
+ Watch your urine: pale straw-yellow = fine; dark and little = drink!

## 4.3 | Electrolytes
Minerals that split into ions in water – key to cell function and nerve-muscle signalling. Under heavy exertion, extreme temperatures and little water, keeping them balanced is vital.
- **Sodium (Na⁺):** blood pressure, blood volume, muscle and nerve function; lost quickly in sweat. Sources: salt, salty food, electrolyte drinks.
- **Potassium (K⁺):** heart rhythm, muscle function. Sources: bananas, dried fruit, field rations.
- **Calcium (Ca²⁺):** muscle contraction, bones, blood clotting. Sources: milk powder, tinned food, supplements.
- **Magnesium (Mg²⁺):** nerve-muscle function, prevents cramps. Sources: seeds, grains.
- **Chloride (Cl⁻):** acid–base balance, digestion. Source: table salt.
- **Bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻):** blood pH. Made by the body, can be replaced with solutions.
**Causes of loss:** heavy sweating (heat, heavy kit), diarrhoea, vomiting (contaminated water), injuries, burns, prolonged lack of water.
**Signs of deficiency:** muscle cramps, weakness, dizziness; irregular heartbeat, low blood pressure; poor concentration; in severe cases loss of consciousness.

## 4.4 | Replacing electrolytes
- Replace water and electrolytes **together** – drinking only water while sweating can cause hyponatraemia.
- Isotonic drink powders in MREs are absorbed quickly.
- Salt tablets: in extreme heat, in the desert.
- Survival mix: **½ teaspoon of salt / 1 litre of water**.
- Dried fruit, nuts, dried meat, energy bars – potassium, magnesium.
**Mandatory items of a military survival kit:** electrolyte drink powder, salt tablets, water purification tablets, compact water purifier.

## 4.5 | Water intoxication (hyponatraemia)
A life-threatening condition: too much water in a short time while the blood's sodium level drops – cells (including brain cells) swell, causing brain oedema and even death.
**Causes:** the kidneys can't excrete more than about 0.8–1.0 l/hour; extreme exertion with over-hydration; no electrolyte replacement; stress, compulsive drinking.
**Prevention:**
- In moderation: max. **0.5–0.8 litres/hour**, spread evenly.
- Replace electrolytes in heat and on long missions.
- Listen to thirst – don't drink compulsively “in advance”, but do drink regularly.
! Water is a strategic resource: both waste and over-drinking are harmful.

## 4.6 | Thickening of the blood
Haemoconcentration depends not only on water intake but also on fluid loss and electrolyte balance.
**Causes:** large fluid loss (sweat, breath, urine, diarrhoea); heat + exertion without salt and water; severe dehydration (thrombosis risk); too much salt or protein.
**The right balance:** water + electrolytes together; under exertion 0.5–0.8 l/hour, in heat salt tablets or electrolyte powder.

## 4.7 | Finding water sources | web
Always look for the cleanest source first: **spring > running water > still water**.
- Valley bottoms, green vegetation at the foot of rocks, reeds, sedges, willow and alder indicate water.
- Game trails going downhill often lead to water; swarming insects, birds at dawn and dusk.
- Dig on the outer bend of a dry stream bed for seeping water.
- Rainwater: collected on a tarp or poncho – the cleanest natural source.
- **Dew:** wipe grass with a cloth at dawn and wring it out.
- Snow and ice: **melt it, don't eat it!** – it chills your body and dries your mouth. Ice gives more water for less fuel.
- **Transpiration bag:** a clear bag over a living, leafy branch in the sun – condensation collects in a few hours.
- **Solar still:** a pit with a container in the middle, plastic sheet over it, weighed down with a stone in the centre.
! Never drink seawater, urine or alcohol – they increase dehydration.
! Avoid water near dead animals, rubbish, industrial sites or farmland, and water that is foamy, oily, smelly or lifeless.

## 4.8 | Filtering and clarifying | web
Filtering removes cloudiness but **does not disinfect** – always purify afterwards.
1. Settling: let it stand, then gently pour off the top.
2. Improvised filter (bottle, sock, trouser leg), layers from top to bottom: grass/cloth → fine sand → charcoal (from the fire, not ash) → gravel.
3. Pouring through cloth (scarf, T-shirt) – against larger debris.
+ Charcoal reduces smell and taste but does not kill pathogens.

## 4.9 | Purifying water | web
1. **Boiling – the most reliable:** a rolling boil for at least **1 minute**; above 2000 m, **3 minutes**. Let it cool covered.
2. **Chlorine dioxide tablets** (e.g. Micropur): against bacteria, viruses and Cryptosporidium too. About 30 minutes; with cold or cloudy water up to **4 hours**.
3. **Iodine tablets / tincture:** about 30 minutes; longer in cold water. Not during pregnancy, with thyroid disease, or long-term.
4. **Chlorine bleach (unscented, 5–6%):** 2 drops/litre of clear, 4 drops/litre of cloudy water, 30 minutes; a slight chlorine smell should remain.
5. **Filter bottle, microfilter:** against bacteria and protozoa; against viruses only combined with chemicals.
6. **UV (SODIS):** clear PET bottle, all day in strong sun – only as a last resort.
! After treating with tablets, loosen the cap and turn the bottle over so the thread is disinfected too.
! Add vitamin C drink powder only after the treatment time – it reduces the effect.

## 4.10 | Storing and rationing | web
- In a clean, closable, labelled container, in the shade; never mix treated and untreated water.
- Don't hoard to the last drop: water is better in your body than in your bottle – but ration it.
- Reduce water loss: rest in the shade during the hottest hours, move at night, don't smoke, talk little, breathe through your nose.
