# C1 | Risk of capture and how to prevent it | CHARLIE · based on AFH 10-644 and public NATO sources

## C1.1 | Who is level C for? | web
Level C is for **high-risk** personnel: those whose task, post or rank makes isolation and capture likely, and who carry information of special value to the enemy.
- Aircrew, special operations forces, reconnaissance, long-range patrols.
- Liaison and monitoring teams, personnel serving abroad, senior personnel.
- In peacetime too: the risk of hostage-taking or arbitrary detention abroad.
! The core of level C is practical training under controlled conditions with experienced instructors. This chapter gives the theory only; it does not replace the course.

## C1.2 | Preparation before you go | web
- **ISOPREP:** fill it in and keep it up to date; rescuers use these personal questions to authenticate you.
- **Evasion plan of action (EPA):** route, rally points, signals, time windows – in your head, not on paper.
- **Personal items:** only what the task needs. Don't take letters, photos, notes or unnecessary documents; your phone should hold nothing that exposes you, your family or your unit.
- **Survival kit on your body:** if you lose your pack or vehicle, this stays with you.
- **Fitness and health:** captivity and evasion test the body too.

## C1.3 | The moment of capture | web
Capture is one of the most dangerous phases: the captors are tense and the situation is chaotic.
- If resistance is hopeless, **don't provoke**: hands visible, slow movements, follow instructions.
- Stay calm and observe: how many there are, where they take you, what you see and hear – you may need this to escape later.
- Ask for care for yourself and your wounded comrades.
- Don't start conversations, don't speculate aloud, don't make threats.
+ How you behave in the first hours matters: a calm, disciplined prisoner attracts less violence.

## C1.4 | Escape in the early phase | web
According to public doctrine, the best chance to escape is often **soon after capture**: friendly forces are still close, the captors are disorganised, and you are still at your strongest.
- Weigh it up: how good the chance is, your health, where you would go, and what failure would mean – for you and your comrades.
- If you go: at once, decisively, then apply the rules of evasion (B4).
- If not: observe, gather information, save your strength for a later opportunity.
