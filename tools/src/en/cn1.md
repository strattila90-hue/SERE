# N1 | Nuclear situations and doctrine | EXTRA · based on public sources

## N1.1 | What is a nuclear doctrine? | web
A **nuclear doctrine** is a state's public set of rules on when, why and on whose authority it may use nuclear weapons. Its main aim is **deterrence**: the other side should not dare to attack, because the response would cause unacceptable damage.
- **NATO:** the Alliance states that as long as nuclear weapons exist, it will remain a nuclear alliance. Its deterrence rests on the nuclear forces of the USA, the UK and France, and on nuclear sharing.
- **Russia:** the new doctrine approved on 19 November 2024 replaces the earlier "threat to the very existence of the state" with a **critical threat to sovereignty or territorial integrity** as a possible reason.
- The same document treats an attack by a non-nuclear state supported by a nuclear state as a **joint attack**, and extends deterrence to states that provide territory for an attack.
> A doctrine is a political signal. As soldiers and civilians, our job is not to guess, but to know what to do if trouble comes anyway.
+ Doctrines change – always check the current texts and official information.

## N1.2 | Effects of a nuclear explosion | web
1. **Flash:** blinding light that can cause temporary blindness for up to a minute.
2. **Blast wave:** can cause fatal injuries and building damage for kilometres. It arrives seconds after the flash, later at greater distances.
3. **Heat and fire:** burns and fires over a radius of kilometres.
4. **Initial radiation:** at the moment of the explosion, mainly close by.
5. **Electromagnetic pulse (EMP):** can destroy electronics; causes temporary disruption further away.
6. **Radioactive fallout:** contaminated dust and debris carried high up falls back down. It is dangerous to **people outdoors**, and usually reaches more distant areas **more than 15 minutes** after the explosion.
! The best protection against fallout is thick walls and earth: the more material between you and the open sky, the lower the radiation.

## N1.3 | At the moment of the flash | web
1. **Don't look at the light.** Turn away and close your eyes.
2. **Lie face down** behind cover (a wall, ditch or hole), so as little skin as possible is exposed.
3. Cover your head and neck with your arms. Don't touch your eyes, nose or mouth.
4. **Stay down until the blast wave has passed** – this can take seconds, and a return wind may follow.
5. **In a vehicle:** stop safely and duck below the level of the seats.
6. If you are warned in advance: go into the nearest building immediately and keep away from windows.

## N1.4 | Get inside, stay inside, stay tuned | web
> GET INSIDE · STAY INSIDE · STAY TUNED
**Get inside:** after the blast wave you have **about 10 minutes or more** to find shelter before fallout arrives. Vehicles, tents and lightweight houses do not protect you.
- Best place: a **basement**, or the **middle** of a large brick or concrete building, away from outer walls and the roof.
- In a tall building, the inner part of the middle floors is better than the top floor or the windowed part of the ground floor.
**Stay inside:** for at least **24 hours** unless the authorities say otherwise. Don't set off to fetch your family – everyone should stay where they are and meet later. Keep pets inside too.
**Stay tuned:** a battery or wind-up radio works even when the mobile network, TV and internet are down. The authorities will say when and where it is safe to go out.
+ Close doors and windows, and switch off ventilation and air conditioning that draws air from outside.

## N1.5 | The 7:10 rule | web
Radiation from fallout drops quickly. A simple rule of thumb: **every sevenfold increase in time cuts the radiation to a tenth.**
- Example: if it is 400 units **2 hours** after the explosion, it is about 40 after **14 hours**, and about 4 two days later.
- That is why **the first hours are the most dangerous** – shelter matters most then.
! A rule of thumb, not a measurement. A radiation meter is more accurate if you have one and know how to use it – base your decision on official instructions.

## N1.6 | Decontamination: if you were outside | web
1. Before or straight after entering the building, **take off your outer layer of clothing**, put it in a bag, tie it and put it away from people.
2. **Shower** with lukewarm water and soap. If you can't: wipe exposed skin and hair with a clean, damp cloth.
3. **Don't scrub** the skin hard, and don't use hand sanitiser or disinfectant wipes to remove fallout.
4. Brush pets gently, then wash them with soap and water.
5. Put on clean clothes that were stored indoors.

## N1.7 | Food, water, health | web
- **Packaged** food and drink, and food and drink **stored indoors**, are safe. Wipe the packaging before opening.
- Don't eat or drink anything that was **left uncovered outdoors**.
- Go for medical care when the authorities say so; in a public shelter, tell staff at once if you are ill or injured.
- **Shelter supplies** for at least 24 hours, preferably 3 days: water, food, medicines, a battery or wind-up radio, torch, batteries, soap, wet wipes.

## N1.8 | Nuclear power plant accident in Hungary | web
In Hungary the **National Nuclear Emergency Response System (ONER)** monitors radiation, runs the alert system and decides on protecting the population.
**Alerts:** sirens, radio, television, local loudspeakers. If you hear a siren: go inside and turn on the radio or TV.
**Sheltering at home:**
1. Come inside, keep the family together, bring pets in too.
2. Close doors, windows and vents; seal gaps if needed.
3. Turn on the radio or TV and follow the instructions.
4. Get out the information leaflet you received earlier and prepare for further instructions.
**Iodine tablets:** only take them if the authorities **order it** – they only protect against radioactive iodine, not other radiation. The authorities will say where they are handed out and how to take them.
! Don't take iodine tablets "just in case" on your own decision.
**Evacuation:** only when instructed. Take: personal documents, medicines, mobile phone with charger, warm clothes. Leave by your own car, or if you have none, by the buses sent to your settlement.
+ Official radiation data: the disaster management radiation monitoring site and the gamma dose rate map of the Hungarian Meteorological Service.

## N1.9 | As a soldier in the field | web
- **Cover first:** the best protection from fallout is a place dug into the ground and covered from above (cellar, bunker, covered trench). Open ground and vehicles do not protect you.
- **Don't move in the first hours** if you have cover – by the 7:10 rule, waiting greatly reduces your radiation dose.
- **Follow CBRN procedures** and your commander's orders: protective equipment, decontamination, marking and avoiding contaminated areas.
- Keep water and food clean: only use sealed packs that have been wiped.
- **Report** any contamination you find and any casualties.
! This is a practice aid – it does not replace the current CBRN regulations and training.
