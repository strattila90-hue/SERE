# 1 | The SERE training system | Part I, Lesson 1

## 1.1 | Introduction
Our world is becoming ever more civilised, and in the process the instincts needed for survival are fading. To protect soldiers, special attention must be paid to restoring and developing their ability to survive.
SERE is an English acronym: **S**urvival, **E**vasion, **R**esistance, **E**xtraction.
The training covers tactics, techniques and procedures that reduce the chance of an isolated person being captured and significantly improve their chances of staying alive in different environments. If captured, they can resist and survive captivity and, if the situation allows, escape.

## 1.2 | About this course
The unified SERE training system ensures that everyone knows the SERE rules in the detail required for their level – “A”, “B” or “C” – so that the risk of losing or having personnel captured is reduced if they become isolated.
> Theory is essential, but it never replaces practice. Hear it and you forget; see it and you know it; do it and you can do it!
- Low risk category → SERE “A”
- Medium risk category → SERE “B”
- High risk category → SERE “C”

## 1.3 | SERE training in general
In the Hungarian Defence Forces, the SERE training system is regulated by a directive of the Chief of Defence.

## 1.4 | Key terms
- **ISOP – Isolated Personnel:** a soldier or civilian separated from their unit or organisation who has lost positive and/or procedural control and must use survival, evasion and resistance techniques in order to return.
- **Survival:** all activities that allow the isolated person to stay alive and maintain physical and mental health under various environmental conditions.
- **Evasion:** procedures that allow the isolated person to avoid detection and contact by hostile or potentially hostile forces and people, or to enable return.
- **Resistance:** tactics, techniques and procedures that allow the isolated person, if captured, to reduce the effectiveness of the captors' interrogation tactics.
- **Extraction:** recovering the isolated person from hostile or potentially hostile territory.
- **JPR – Joint Personnel Recovery:** the sum of military, diplomatic and civil efforts to recover and reintegrate isolated personnel.

## 1.5 | Principles
Recent operations show a significant chance that both soldiers and civilians become isolated, are captured and face coercive interrogation. Participants must be able to survive, evade the enemy, prepare for extraction or exfiltrate back to friendly forces; if necessary resist coercive interrogation and assist in their own recovery, including escape from captivity.
Because modern battlefields are vast and technology advances, our troops operate at ever greater distances, which increases the chance of isolation. ISOP must be prepared for recovery and self-recovery, trained, and equipped with the necessary gear.
Losing soldiers trained on advanced equipment can mean a decisive loss of capability, so they must be prepared to survive, to exfiltrate (escape) or to prepare for extraction.
Most theatres have a JPR system, whose effectiveness depends not only on equipment but also on how well prepared the person to be recovered is.

## 1.6 | The SERE training system
Training can be theoretical or practical depending on the level, environment, threat and theatre. Training should be carried out with exactly the equipment that will be used on operations. Success depends on uniform training, preparation and proper equipment – their absence can even be a strategic risk.
Anything not in line with the valid SERE training programme cannot be considered SERE training. The programme is prepared by the permanent SERE group and approved by the training directorate.
Nationally the system follows NATO STANAG 7196 (ED 2) – SERE Training Standard, APRP-3.3.7.5 Edition B.

## 1.7 | Levels of the training programme
- **SERE “A”:** theoretical training for personnel at low risk of isolation; delivered by the units' training staff.
- **SERE “B”:** theoretical and practical training for medium-risk personnel; delivered by the central SERE training group with the units.
- **SERE “C”:** training for high-risk personnel; coordinated by the subject-matter lead and delivered by the central SERE training group.
+ Levels “B” and “C” are valid for 5 years and can be extended by 3 years with refresher training – any number of times.

## 1.8 | The SERE “A” course
SERE “A” is theoretical training for personnel at low risk of isolation. Each chapter takes about 1–2 hours and ends with a 10-question test; the pass mark is 70%.
