# 8 | What is expected of the person to be recovered | Part III, Lesson 2 — based on the course outline, extended from public sources

## 8.1 | Staying alive | web
The isolated person's needs in order – the SERE priorities:
1. **Personal protection:** first aid, shelter, clothing, fire – against weather and enemy.
2. **Sustenance – water and food:** water comes before food.
3. **Health – physical and psychological:** treating injuries, hygiene, sleep, morale, the will to survive.
4. **Travel:** only with good reason and a plan – either wait for rescue on the spot, or evade/exfiltrate.
+ Memory aid: the **“rule of threes”** – 3 minutes without air, 3 hours unprotected in extreme weather, 3 days without water, 3 weeks without food.

## 8.2 | Keeping your dignity | web
- **Captivity – worse than evasion!** Seize every opportunity to avoid capture.
- **Resistance:** in captivity the isolated person follows the code of conduct: gives only the details required by international law (Geneva Conventions) – name, rank, date of birth, service number; does not cooperate with the enemy, makes no propaganda statements.
- **Escape:** if an opportunity arises and the risk is acceptable, it is a duty to try to escape – the first hours after capture are the most favourable.
+ Faith, comradeship, routine and discipline: the foundations of surviving captivity mentally.

## 8.3 | Preparing before deployment | web
- Fill in and update your ISOPREP; know the EPA (evasion plan).
- Comms plan: frequencies, call signs, codes, check-in windows.
- Check the survival kit and signalling devices on your body.
- Know the area: map, extraction sites, rally points, danger areas.

## 8.4 | Reporting the isolating event | web
- Report as soon as you can: who, where, when, condition, intentions, threat.
- With a radio: a short, coded transmission in the given window from a concealed spot – then move.
- Without a radio: use signalling devices at the right time at the extraction site.

## 8.5 | Informing the recovery forces | web
- Exact position (grid), how to authenticate, injuries, enemy forces, the terrain and the landing site.
- Listen and follow instructions; don't make unnecessary transmissions.

## 8.6 | The recovery site | web
- Concealed close to the designated site, with enough open ground for the recovery asset.
- An obstacle-free landing site (for helicopters: no trees, wires or loose objects); wind direction.
- Signal only when the recovery force approaches / on instruction.

## 8.7 | Physical contact in brief | web
1. Signal as instructed.
2. Weapon on safe, hands visible, don't run toward the recovery asset.
3. Authenticate yourself (ISOPREP questions).
4. Accept being searched, follow the recovery force's instructions.
5. When boarding, follow the recovery force's commands – head down, away from the rotor.
