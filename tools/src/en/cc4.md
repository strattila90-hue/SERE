# C4 | Escape, extraction and reintegration | CHARLIE · based on AFH 10-644 and public NATO sources

## C4.1 | The decision to escape | web
- **When?** The earlier, the better the chance – but only if the situation is favourable. Plan a later escape thoroughly.
- **Weigh up:** physical condition, knowledge of the terrain, where friendly forces are, what happens to your comrades if you leave.
- **Preparation:** gather information (guard changes, terrain, weather), save strength and, if possible, food.
- **Afterwards:** apply the rules of evasion at once (B4) – move at night, hide your tracks, make contact according to the plan.
! A failed escape can have serious consequences. The decision is your responsibility, with the senior prisoner's knowledge if possible.

## C4.2 | Extraction and rescue | web
- Rescue forces work fast and decisively and treat everyone as dangerous until identified.
- **Don't run toward them.** Lie down or stay where you are, hands visible, don't grab anything.
- Follow instructions to the letter; accept being searched and restrained.
- Authentication: answer the ISOPREP questions, tell them where other comrades are.
+ At the moment of rescue, discipline saves lives – yours too.

## C4.3 | Reintegration | web
Release is not the end of the story. The reintegration process in NATO countries has several phases:
1. **Medical care and rest:** assessing your physical condition, treatment.
2. **Debriefing:** what you know may help those still in captivity and future training.
3. **Psychological support:** a specialist helps you process what happened.
4. **Gradual return** to family and duty.
- Sleep problems, irritability, guilt or withdrawal lasting for months are normal. They can be treated – ask for and accept help.

## C4.4 | What you can do now | web
- Fill in and update your ISOPREP; know your communications and evasion plan by heart.
- Keep up your fitness and survival knowledge (levels A and B).
- Put your papers in order, tell your family who to contact if something happens.
- Practise stress management: breathing, routine, small goals.
- Apply for an official level B or C course if your post justifies it.
