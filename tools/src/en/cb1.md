# B1 | Advanced fire, shelter and weather | BRAVO · based on ATP 3-50.21 and AFH 10-644

## B1.1 | Lighting a fire without matches or a lighter | web
At level B one lighter is not enough: have at least two other ways to make fire, and practise them in dry and wet weather.
- **Ferrocerium rod:** strike down with the knife spine or a scraper, right above the tinder; works in rain too.
- **Magnifier, glasses, clear bottle of water:** focus sunlight on one point and dark tinder will ignite.
- **Battery + steel wool:** the two terminals of a 9 V battery, or two AA cells in series, instantly heat fine steel wool.
- **Bow drill:** dry softwood (lime, poplar, willow), a notch in the fireboard, steady and accelerating strokes – takes a lot of practice.
**Tinder in wet weather:** the inside of standing deadwood, feather sticks (thin shavings left attached to the stick), birch bark (oily, burns even when wet), resinous pine stumps.
! One fire starter is no fire starter. Keep several methods in your survival kit, waterproofed, on your body.

## B1.2 | Fire types by task | web
- **Teepee:** catches quickly; for cooking and signalling.
- **Log cabin (criss-crossed sticks):** makes plenty of coals and holds heat; for cooking.
- **Star (logs radiating, burning in the middle):** burns slowly and economically; push the logs in during the night.
- **Dakota fire hole:** two connected holes (fire pit + air tunnel); low flame, little smoke and light – a concealed fire.
- **Reflector wall:** logs or stones behind the fire; directs heat toward the shelter.
+ In hostile territory: small, dry hardwood, under the canopy; smoke gives you away by day, light by night. Don't light a fire unless you must.

## B1.3 | Advanced shelters | web
- **Lean-to + reflector wall:** back to the wind, fire in front, wall behind the fire – the warmest simple solution.
- **Debris hut:** ridgepole and ribs covered with 60–90 cm of dry leaves; warm even without fire, only slightly bigger than your body.
- **Tarp A-frame:** cord between two trees, tarp stretched over it; quick, best in rain.
- **Snow trench and snow cave:** snow is a good insulator. Entrance lower than the sleeping platform (cold air drains out), always a ventilation hole, keep the digging tool inside.
- **Hammock, raised bed:** over wet ground or ground full of creatures.
! A fire or stove burning in a snow cave or tent: carbon monoxide poisoning. Always ventilate!

## B1.4 | Cold and heat injuries | web
**Hypothermia** signs: violent shivering, then clumsiness, stumbling, mumbling, confusion, and finally shivering stops – this is already life-threatening.
- Action: sheltered dry place, insulation below and above, dry clothes, a warm (not hot) sweet drink if conscious, body-to-body warmth.
**Frostbite:** white, waxy, numb skin.
- Don't rub it, don't warm it by the fire. Only rewarm with body heat if it can't refreeze.
**Heat exhaustion:** pale, clammy skin, weakness, headache → shade, rest, water + salt.
**Heat stroke:** hot red skin, confusion, unconsciousness → **cool immediately** (water, wet cloth, wind); life-threatening.
**Trench foot:** feet wet and cold for days. Prevention: dry socks for the night, daily foot care.
